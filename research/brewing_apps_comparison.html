Field Notes for a Local-First Brewing App

The Brewing
Software Landscape

Every existing tool falls into one of four camps. Nobody is doing local-first plus transparent formulas. That's the wedge — and here's the map of the territory.

The Competitors

Most brewing apps live in the cloud, paywall their recipe caps, and treat their calculation engine as a black box. BeerSmith just (March 2026) added a local-mode toggle in v4, which is a tell — the market is asking for it. The open-source locals exist but look like they were designed in 2008. Recipes move between tools via BeerXML, the de facto interchange format.

The Opening

Two holes in the market, both unclaimed. Hit either one and you're differentiated; hit both and you own a positioning nobody else has.

Local-first is underserved

Brewfather (the mindshare leader) caps free accounts and requires the cloud. Brewer's Friend and Grainfather are cloud-only. BeerSmith 4 just added a local toggle last month — the signal is clear. The open-source locals exist but look like 2008-era Qt desktop apps with no mobile story. A modern, local-first app with a good UI is a real opening.

Formula transparency is nobody's pitch

Every app hides the math. Users pick equations from dropdowns (Tinseth vs. Rager for IBUs) but nobody exposes, documents, or lets you edit the actual formulas. For the Palmer-reading, hop-utilization- arguing segment of homebrewers, “all the math is visible and editable” is a real hook. Nobody is claiming it.

The AI Arrival

A year ago this space had no AI. Now it's table stakes — and the shift landed last month. If you're entering in 2026, you don't get to ignore this question.

New
Mar 2026
Brewfather shipped an AI Brewing Assistant inside a new Premium Plus tier, above their regular ~$30/year Premium. It generates recipes from descriptions, modifies existing ones, and references your inventory — all via monthly AI credits. With ~200k users and a history of flat, single-tier pricing, breaking out a higher tier specifically for AI is the clearest possible demand signal. Assume BeerSmith, Brewer's Friend, and Grainfather follow within 12 months.

Translation: the whole homebrew-software pie — if someone captured 100% of the non-BeerSmith, non-Brewfather segment and charged everyone — is maybe $5M/year globally. A realistic, well-executed entrant might reach 5–10k paying users in three years. At $12/yr that's roughly $60–120k/year in ARR. That's a respectable side income and a badge of honor; it is not a career-replacing business.

The one exception is the commercial brewery upmarket path (9,922 US breweries with real software budgets, think $1k–5k ACV). Totally different product, though — that's brewery ERP, not a recipe calculator. Brewken is the only entrant publicly heading there, and it's struggling. File it under "possible future pivot, not v1."

Monetization: Three Plays, Ranked

Given the small TAM, incumbent lock-in, and your stated values (local-first, transparent formulas), here are the three viable paths with honest trade-offs.

Model How it works Pros Cons
Open core +
paid sync		 App is free and open-source. Local-first, all features unlocked, no account required. Optional paid sync (~$10–15/yr) handles cross-device encrypted sync & cloud backup. The Obsidian model. Aligns philosophically with transparent formulas. Zero acquisition friction. Sync is an honest pay-for (you run servers). Low maintenance if you don't offer collab features. Revenue is real but modest. You still have to operate sync infrastructure. Conversion is usually 3–8% of active users, so revenue scales slowly. Not a primary-income path.
Pure FOSS +
donations		 Fully open, MIT/GPL, local-only. No paid tier. Optional GitHub Sponsors / Patreon / "buy me a beer" button. Sync via user-provided Dropbox/iCloud/Git (the free-Obsidian workaround). Maximum ideological alignment. Zero server costs. Community contributions possible. You can sleep at night and never think about churn. Revenue is a tip jar, realistically $0–5k/yr even at scale. No meaningful incentive structure. Burnout risk on solo maintenance with no funding.
Freemium
(Brewfather-style)		 Closed-source. Free tier with recipe caps; Premium (~$20–30/yr) for unlimited recipes, sync, imports. Highest revenue ceiling of the three. Conventional, understood model. Directly competes with Brewfather on its own terms — and loses. Clashes hard with "local-first + transparent formulas" positioning. Recipe caps are the #1 user complaint about Brewfather; replicating them is suicidal.
The Honest Take

Ship it FOSS with optional paid sync. And size expectations.

Your two stated differentiators — local-first and transparent formulas — are ideological claims. Going closed-source with a freemium paywall would undercut both. Open-source the app. All of it. Let the formulas be inspectable in code, which is the strongest possible version of "our math is transparent."

Charge for optional encrypted sync at $10–15/year. It's the honest pay-for: you run servers, users who want convenience across devices pay you for it, and users who don't can use Dropbox, iCloud, or Git for free. This is exactly Obsidian's model, which funds a small team on a niche product — and Obsidian has a much bigger TAM than you do.

Set expectations accordingly: this is a craft project that might earn modest side income, not a startup. The brewing-software market is too small and too locked-in for a winner-take-most outcome. But if you ship something genuinely good, 2–5k delighted users paying $12/year is achievable, and that's a real thing to have built.

Name availability · verified

Trub is open.

No brewing software uses the name. The closest hit is Trubble Brewing, a physical brewpub in Fort Wayne, Indiana — different spelling, different category, different trademark class. A few things worth knowing before you commit:

  • Pronunciation is contested — US says “truhb,” DE says “troob.” Pick one for your branding.
  • The connotation cuts both ways — trub is the sediment, the leftovers. Homebrewers get the joke instantly; outsiders need a tagline.
  • Domain strategy: trub.com is almost certainly taken. Try trub.app, trub.beer, gettrub.com, trub.dev.
  • Mild SEO noise from Trubble Brewing, but you're in different product classes — low risk.
